# Api yang Kita Nyalakan Sendiri

## What Lies Beneath — Makna yang Tersembunyi

At first glance, Desire sounds like a song about a burning want. But the lyrics under analysis care more about what happens after the fire starts: who lit it, who kept feeding it, and who ends up burning. The central conflict is not between the narrator and the outside world. It is between two versions of the self, "all the things I'm not" and "all the things I am", which keep colliding without ever being reconciled.

The narrator's perspective shifts from putting questions to a "you" to a bitter admission that there is no one else to blame. That "you" can be read as another person, but also as a voice inside the narrator's own head. The lyrics do not rule out either. The meaning is that desire is not just a pull toward something out there. It is an engine that drives its owner and consumes them at once. The song does not judge desire as bad. It only refuses to pretend that desire has no price.

## Following the Feeling — Perjalanan Perasaan

### The Fire Beyond the Hills: An Attraction Still Far Away

The song opens with an almost mythic image: a fire over the arching hills, and then a single word to name it, desire. The fire is still in the distance, and the narrator is not yet inside it. The question "Did you ever stop to think / What all of this could bring to you?" gives the opening a cautious tone. Before moving closer, there is an invitation to count what might be gained and, implicitly, what might be lost.

### A Mind on Fire: Inspiration and Chaos

In the second verse, the fire moves from the horizon into the head. Crossed wires and firing synapses describe a state that is both thrilling and chaotic: unmistakably inspired, yet short-circuiting. The line "All you needed was some time to think" sounds like advice that arrives too late or is ignored, because room for clear thought is hardest to find just when desire is at its peak.

### The Colliding Chorus: Contradiction Without an Answer

In the chorus, the mood turns from fervor to doubt. "Why do you always seem to contradict me?" and "did I ever really have a shot?" show the narrator beginning to question not only the object of desire, but also the right to want it at all. The word "mystery" suggests the source of the conflict is not fully understood. It is the gap between self-image and reality that makes the chance feel like something never truly held.

### Hiding the Feeling, Closing the Door on Blame

The third verse reveals a defensive strategy: fate runs freely across the open plain while the narrator chooses to hide their feelings, reasoning that "there ain't much to gain". The logic is cold and transactional. Then comes the admission that this is a "shameful game" with no one else to blame. The shift from asking to admitting is the emotional turning point of the song, the moment the narrator stops looking for causes outside the self.

### A Mantra of Fire and Ash

The bridge repeats the same four lines again and again, until it feels like a mantra or a loop that cannot be stopped. The sequence reads as a complete chain of cause and effect: fuel, fire, pyre, emptiness. "Self-made" makes explicit the responsibility that was avoided earlier. If the vocal credits on the supplied lyric sheet are taken as a clue, the arrival of a second voice here can be read as two sides of the self finally stating the same conclusion. That is a reading, not a fact about the creators' intent.

### Back to the Same Question

The final chorus and the outro return to the opening question with no new answer. Having understood how the fire works, the narrator does not step out of the circle but simply sees it more clearly. The song ends on the word "collide", so the circular feeling remains: understanding does not automatically lead to change.

## Symbols & Metaphors — Simbolisme dan Makna Figuratif

- **The fire beyond the hills**
  - Symbolic meaning: desire as something beautiful that calls from a distance.
  - Function in the song: it opens the story and sets the initial distance between the narrator and the object of want.
  - Link to the narrator's experience: the distance leaves room for hope before the consequences are felt.
- **Crossed wires and firing synapses**
  - Symbolic meaning: inspiration mixed with mental chaos, when ideas arrive faster than they can be weighed.
  - Function in the song: it moves the fire from the outer landscape into the head.
  - Link to the narrator's experience: desire is no longer scenery but a condition that takes over their thinking.
- **The open plain and fleeing fate**
  - Symbolic meaning: wide but ungraspable opportunity, while the narrator chooses to hide.
  - Function in the song: a contrast between openness outside and silence inside.
  - Link to the narrator's experience: it suggests the chance was not closed off by others, but lost because feelings were concealed.
- **Fuel and the "self-made pyre"**
  - Symbolic meaning: the parts of the self fed to desire, and the stack that ends up as one's own site of burning.
  - Function in the song: the core of the bridge, uniting cause, process, and result in a single image.
  - Link to the narrator's experience: an admission that they helped stack the wood they are burning on.
- **Collision ("collide")**
  - Symbolic meaning: the clash between the imagined self and the real one.
  - Function in the song: the closing word, which keeps the conflict open.
  - Link to the narrator's experience: the source of the doubt in the chorus, never truly resolved.

## An Alternative Reading — Pembacaan Alternatif

The song can also be read as a reflection on ambition or creative drive. The language of the second verse, "unmistakably inspired", "synapses fire", "vivid thoughts", and the need for time to think, sounds like the process of making something or chasing something large. In this reading, "did I ever really have a shot?" is the doubt of a dreamer about their own worthiness. "The emptiness after desire" describes the hollowness when the pursuit is over, whether because it was achieved or because the energy ran out.

A relational reading is also possible, since the chorus addresses a "you" who keeps contradicting the narrator. But the lyrics name no particular relationship, so that reading is best held loosely. All three can coexist: desire for a person, for an achievement, and for a better version of the self can each be a fire one lights oneself.

## The Aftertone — Gema yang Tersisa

Desire does not end with a lesson. It ends with an image that is hard to shake: someone standing before a fire and realizing they helped stack the wood. There is no accusation aimed at the world, no redemption promised, only the uncomfortable honesty that our biggest wants often demand the biggest share of us.

A fire we light ourselves will always ask us to decide what is worth burning, and what is not.
