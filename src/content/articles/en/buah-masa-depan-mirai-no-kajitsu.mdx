# Benih yang Ditanam Sebelum Kita Siap

## What Lies Beneath — Makna yang Tersembunyi

There's a patch of soil behind an old building. For years it was left alone, overgrown with weeds nobody asked for. One day, someone is handed a packet of seeds and asked to plant them. They hesitate, not because the soil is bad, but because they feel **not worthy yet** of holding something that can grow.

> Readiness is often not a condition for starting, but a gift that arrives afterward.

We tend to believe the person who deserves to plant is the one who already understands everything. But soil never asks about that. It simply receives what's planted, then works in silence. This story asks gently: **who is really waiting for whom, the seed for us, or us for ourselves to feel enough?**

## Following the Feeling — Perjalanan Perasaan

### 1. A Patch of Silent Soil

Someone stands at the edge of that bare ground, looking at it for a long time. The soil doesn't demand anything, doesn't promise anything. It simply exists, wide and a little frightening because there are **too many possibilities**. That person grips their seeds tightly and thinks: maybe the heaviest part of starting is realizing that no one will do it for us.

### 2. A Small Seed, a Big Doubt

The seed weighs almost nothing in the palm. But the doubt inside the head weighs many times more. Then someone nearby, no one in particular, throws out something funny and everyone laughs. The laughter spreads, and suddenly people are running together toward the end of the patch, to a low wall with an open sky behind it. **They don't yet know what's there, but running toward it feels right.** There's a simple wisdom here: sometimes we move first and understand why later.

### 3. A Heat That Tests

The following days are hot. Water has to be carried from far away, skin turns red, bodies tire. The routine starts to feel dull. But that's where something works slowly: **the unseen diligence before the first result appears.** Then one tiny sprout pushes up, and everyone stops talking. That sprout isn't the result, but **proof that all the tiredness wasn't wasted**.

### 4. Storm and Roots

Then a storm arrives and knocks almost everything down. Someone stands before the wrecked patch, crying quietly. It feels fair to ask, what was all this for if it ends up destroyed? But someone older, who has tended soil for a long time, says briefly that without storms the roots get lazy about going down. The sentence hangs there, and the next day they plant again. **Some things only grow deep in the soil after the soil has been shaken.**

### 5. A Smile That Moves

One morning, someone who's usually afraid to stand out smiles first at a stranger who's usually glum. The stranger is startled, then smiles back. The next day, the stranger comes carrying something to add to the garden. A few days later, other people come too. **One small gesture, with no intention behind it, passes from one face to another.** We rarely realize how large the effect of something we consider trivial can be.

### 6. A Small Harvest and a Day Unnoticed

Months later, the first fruit appears. Small, sour, far from impressive. But they share it piece by piece until everyone gets some, and it tastes better than anything. That afternoon, the wind from beyond the wall blows softly, and no one knows that this ordinary day will someday be what they miss. **The most precious time often disguises itself as an ordinary day.** From that fruit, they keep seeds for someone else to plant.

## Symbols & Metaphors — Simbolisme dan Makna Figuratif

- **The Soil Left Idle (The Idle Soil):** It demands nothing and promises nothing. Soil teaches that a place that looks empty isn't necessarily useless, only not yet given a chance.
- **A Packet of Seeds (The Seed):** Light in the hand, heavy in the mind. It reminds us that the start of something big is often very small, and almost always unconvincing.
- **Water and Leaves (The Water and the Leaves):** A quiet, repeating routine. This is where faithfulness is tested, not in the big moments but in the boring days.
- **Heat and Storm (The Heat and the Storm):** Two things equally unwanted, yet equally at work. One tests patience, the other tests the depth of roots.
- **A Smile (The Smile):** The lightest thing one can give, yet the quickest to travel. It shows that kindness needs no grand reason to spread.
- **The Wind Beyond the Wall (The Wind Beyond the Wall):** It comes from a place they don't yet know. It carries the sense that the future has already begun to be felt, even before it truly arrives.

## An Alternative Reading — Pembacaan Alternatif

Imagine if the soil weren't soil.

Maybe it's **a band** practicing in a dusty storeroom with only three songs. Maybe **a community** that started with five people and slowly grew to fifty. Maybe **a small class** that began with one table and a box of colored pencils.

Or maybe the soil is **oneself**: something left alone for a long time, quietly waiting for someone, often ourselves, to finally dare to plant something inside. Same story, different soils, and all of them make sense.

## The Aftertone — Gema yang Tersisa

If anything in this nameless story felt familiar, it's because it was written for one song: ***Buah Masa Depan (Mirai No Kajitsu)*** by JKT48. The "someone" in the story stands in for the "boys & girls" of that song. The seeds planted in the soil, the water and leaves, the scorching heat and storms that turn out to be necessary, the smile that spreads, running together toward the horizon, and a youth that comes only once and goes unnoticed: all are images from that song, turned into scenes.

The meaning is simple but easy to forget: **we don't have to wait until we feel ready to start planting, and the fruit of hope is only tasted when we plant it together**.

> **Youth doesn't wait for us to be ready; all we can do is plant today, together, and trust the fruit will come.**
